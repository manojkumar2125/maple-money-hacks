Four ways to earn

Flat-rate cards win on simplicity. Category cards win when your spending is predictable. The fee is the whole game.

Flat-rate cashback

1.5–2% on everything

One rate, no categories to track. Best for set-and-forget spenders.

Category cashback

2–5% on your picks

Higher rates on groceries, gas or dining — if your spending is predictable.

Premium + waiver

$99–$240/yr → $0

Big-bank premium cards rebate the fee when you hold their premium chequing account.

Secured on-ramp

from $50 deposit

No credit history needed — your deposit sets the limit while you build a score.

$360 − $240 = $120
vs $270 on free cards

At $1,500/month, Wealthsimple earns $360 at 2% — but the $240 fee leaves just $120 net. Every free card below nets $270 on the same spending. Without the fee waiver, the free cards win. With it, Wealthsimple ties the best free option.

Read this before applying

The earn rate is the headline. These five details decide whether the card actually pays you.

Pay in full, always.

Carrying a balance at 20–21% costs more in a month than a year of cashback earns. Autopay the full statement balance.

Check acceptance.

Amex isn't taken at Costco or Loblaws banners. If a card doesn't work where you shop, its rate is fiction.

Price the annual fee.

A $240 fee needs $1,000/month at 2% just to break even. Without a waiver, free cards win at every spend level.

Watch foreign fees.

Most cards add 2.5% abroad. Wealthsimple charges 0% — on USD spending, that gap matters more than the earn rate.

Some "banks" have no card.

EQ Bank and KOHO don't issue credit cards (prepaid only). If you bank there, pair it with a card from this board.
