  • Simplified model. The 50/30/20 rule is a guideline — adjust the percentages to your real life.
  • Emergency target = 1 month of needs. Many experts suggest 3–6 months once you're past the starter stage.
  • 2026 payroll deductions: CPP 5.95% between $3,500–$74,600 (max $4,230.45) · CPP2 4% between $74,600–$85,000 (max $416) · EI 1.63% up to $68,900 (max $1,123.07). Self-employed and Quebec rates differ and aren't modelled here.
  • 2026 limits: FHSA $8,000/yr ($40,000 lifetime) · TFSA $7,000/yr · RRSP 18% of earned income up to $33,810 · RESP $2,500/yr per child for the full 20% CESG grant ($500).
  • Tax refund estimate = (annual personal RRSP + FHSA contributions) × your Ontario 2026 marginal rate. Your group RRSP match isn't in this refund figure because it's deducted before tax on every paycheque — its tax benefit already shows up as higher take-home pay above, not as a refund at filing time.
  • Employer match math is approximated from your gross salary; your plan's exact rules win.
